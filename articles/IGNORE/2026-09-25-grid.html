The quiet redesign
of the electric grid

More flexible networks are making room for millions of small generators, batteries and homes that can share power.

By Theo MartinPublished September 25, 2026 · 6:15 AM

For most of its history, the grid moved electricity in one direction. The next version looks more like a conversation, with homes and businesses both consuming and returning power.

That redesign is happening in small increments: a neighborhood battery here, a rooftop solar program there, software that helps a utility anticipate demand before the evening rush.

Flexibility is the new capacity

The changes are easy to miss because they do not always require a new power plant. They require better timing, clearer incentives and equipment that can respond when conditions change.

“A resilient grid is one that can make many small decisions well.”Jon Bell, Grid Futures Initiative

For customers, the promise is a system that wastes less energy and stays dependable as weather becomes less predictable.
