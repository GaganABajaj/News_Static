Small models,
serious momentum

The next wave of useful AI may be less about scale and more about fitting intelligence into the places where work actually happens.

By Imani OkaforPublished September 25, 2026 · 6:58 AM

The most interesting AI systems arriving this year are not necessarily the largest ones. They are compact, quick to run and designed around a particular job.

That shift is changing the economics of experimentation. A local model can work with sensitive material without sending it away, respond with less delay and run on hardware already sitting on a desk.

Useful by design

The result is a quieter kind of progress: tools that summarize a meeting while it is happening, spot anomalies in a factory and translate a customer request without asking a remote service for every word.

“The best model is often the one that fits the workflow.”Rina Das, Applied Systems Research

Smaller does not mean simple. It means the design has to be more deliberate, with clear boundaries and a better understanding of the task.
