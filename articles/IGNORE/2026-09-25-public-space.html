Why public space is having
a renaissance

Cities are rediscovering the social value of places that ask nothing from us except that we arrive.

By Celia ParkPublished September 24, 2026 · 5:10 PM

A bench, a shaded corner, a long table in a library: the smallest pieces of a city can create the biggest invitation to belong.

After years of designing public life around speed and transactions, planners are returning attention to the spaces between destinations. The measure is not how efficiently a person passes through, but whether they might choose to stay.

The generous city

That means more than adding trees or repainting a plaza. It means observing how different people share a place, then making room for the unplanned encounters that give a neighborhood its character.

“Good public space lets a city surprise itself.”Nadia Flores, Civic Design Studio

The renaissance is modest by design. It is made from shade, seating, access and time.
