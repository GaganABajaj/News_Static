India Launches Bio‑Based PLA Biodegradable Plastic Pilot under BioE3, Backed by ₹75 Cr Grant

Union Minister Dr. Jitendra Singh formally inaugurated a ₹75 crore, pilot‑scale facility to produce polylactic‑acid (PLA) biodegradable plastic at Balrampur Chini Mills (BCML) in Kumbhi, Uttar Pradesh. Funded through the Biotechnology Industry Research Assistance Council (BIRAC) under the government’s BioE3 framework, the 100 tonnes‑per‑annum (TPA) plant will develop advanced PLA grades and co‑polymers, turning sugar‑derived feedstock into high‑value, eco‑friendly materials and showcasing India’s “waste‑to‑wealth” bio‑economy strategy.

By GaganPublished September 25, 2026 · 10:54 PM
FIELD NOTE / DAILY EDITION

What Happened?

On 24 September 2026, Union Minister of State (Independent Charge) for Science & Technology, Dr. Jitendra Singh officially launched the commercial use of a bio‑based, biodegradable plastic made from polylactic acid (PLA). The ceremony took place at the Department of Biotechnology, Ministry of Science & Technology, New Delhi, and highlighted a new pilot‑scale research‑and‑development (R&D) facility that BCML will operate at its integrated manufacturing complex in Kumbhi, Uttar Pradesh.

Where & When?

  • Location of launch: Department of Biotechnology, New Delhi, India.
  • Location of the pilot plant: Balrampur Chini Mills Limited, Kumbhi, Uttar Pradesh.
  • Date of launch: 24 September 2026 (5:57 PM IST).

Why – The Rationale Behind the Initiative

The project is part of the government’s BioE3 (Biotechnology for Economy, Environment & Employment) framework, aimed at converting India’s diverse biomass waste into valuable products. A ₹75 crore grant from BIRAC (under the Department of Biotechnology) will fund a 100 TPA pilot facility that will:

1. Develop specialized PLA grades and co‑polymers for high‑value applications (e.g., packaging, medical devices). 2. Generate techno‑economic data and process know‑how to accelerate scale‑up to commercial production. 3. Create skilled manpower and foster industry‑academia‑research collaborations.

Minister Singh emphasized that India’s bio‑economy has surged from roughly US$10 billion in 2014 to nearly US$195 billion today, and that moving biotechnology from the lab to the market is essential for sustainable growth, employment generation, and global competitiveness.

Expected Impact & “Waste‑to‑Wealth” Vision

  • Economic: New value chains based on sugarcane and other regional biomass, reducing reliance on petroleum‑based plastics.
  • Environmental: Production of biodegradable plastics that can replace conventional, non‑degradable polymers, lowering plastic pollution.
  • Social: Regional development through localized bioprocessing, upskilling of the workforce, and demonstration of how “there is no such thing as waste.”

The Minister called for broader public outreach to illustrate how everyday agricultural resources like sugarcane can be transformed into sustainable materials, linking the effort to the “Vocal for Local and Global” mantra.

Together they underscored the strategic importance of translating scientific breakthroughs into market‑ready, eco‑friendly products.
