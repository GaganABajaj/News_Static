Supreme Court Petition Seeks Prosecution of CEC Gyanesh Kumar Over SIR Voter‑Roll Controversy

A petition filed under Article 32 of the Indian Constitution asks the Supreme Court to criminally prosecute Chief Election Commissioner Gyanesh Kumar and to quash several decisions made during the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls, alleging unauthorized changes to Form 6, massive voter deletions and illegal software modifications.

By GaganPublished September 26, 2026 · 2:46 PM
AI Image

A petition was lodged on 25 September 2026 in the Supreme Court of India by senior advocate Shailendra Mani Tripathi, invoking Article 32 of the Constitution. The petition targets Chief Election Commissioner (CEC) Gyanesh Kumar, Senior Deputy Election Commissioner Maneesh Garg, Director‑General (IT) Seema Khanna and other officials, seeking criminal prosecution under Section 32 of the Representation of the People Act, 1950 for alleged breaches of statutory duties during the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) of electoral rolls.

The petitioner alleges that CEC Kumar, without the required concurrence of the other two Election Commissioners—Sukhbir Singh Sandhu and Vivek Joshi—unilaterally altered Form 6, centralized voter databases, failed to restore eligible voters via the ECINet system, and authorised the filing of 1.61 million appeals in West Bengal that challenged legitimate voter inclusions. The Indian Express had reported that Sandhu and Joshi recorded at least 14 objections to decisions related to SIR, including the Form 6 changes, which they claimed were made without their knowledge and contrary to the majority opinion required under Section 18 of the Chief Election Commissioner and Other Election Commissioners Act, 2023.

The petition also demands the constitution of an independent Special Investigation Team (SIT) headed by a retired Supreme Court judge, with cybersecurity and IT experts, to conduct a forensic audit of the ECINet and ERONet databases. The SIT is to investigate the deletion of approximately 13 crore (130 million) voter names across 30 states and union territories during the SIR exercise and to identify individual culpability for the alleged unauthorized West Bengal appeals.

In addition to seeking prosecution, the petition asks the Court to declare all decisions, guidelines, directives and software modifications made by CEC Kumar without full commission concurrence as unconstitutional, ultra vires and void ab initio. It further requests the restoration of the original statutory Form 6 as prescribed under the Registration of Electors Rules, 1960, the quashing of the 1.61 million West Bengal appeals filed in the name of the Election Commission, and the preservation of server logs, databases, communications and metadata related to ECINet and ERONet from 1 October 2025 onward pending investigation.

The respondents named in the petition include the Union of India, the Election Commission of India, CEC Gyanesh Kumar, the Cabinet Secretary, Maneesh Garg and Seema Khanna. The case is listed as Shailendra Mani Tripathi v Union of India, Diary No. 59734/2026.
