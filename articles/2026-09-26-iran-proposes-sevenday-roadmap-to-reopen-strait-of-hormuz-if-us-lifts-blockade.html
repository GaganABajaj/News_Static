Iran Proposes Seven‑Day Roadmap to Reopen Strait of Hormuz if U.S. Lifts Blockade

Iran’s foreign minister offered to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and resume nuclear talks within seven days, provided the United States ends its naval blockade, waives oil sanctions and agrees to a regional cease‑fire.

By 10 Minute NewsPublished September 26, 2026 · 2:36 PM

On 25 September 2026, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi announced a seven‑day roadmap aimed at ending the nearly seven‑month U.S.–Iran conflict. The proposal, delivered to reporters outside the UN Security Council in New York, stipulates that if Washington immediately lifts its naval blockade of Iranian ports, releases at least $12 billion of frozen Iranian assets, and waives sanctions on Tehran’s oil sales, Iran will:

  1. Complete initial de‑escalation steps within four to five days.
  2. Reopen the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz on the sixth day, restoring free passage for the roughly one‑fifth of global oil and gas that normally transits the waterway.
  3. Begin formal talks on a final nuclear‑programme agreement on the seventh day.

The plan also calls for a cease‑fire that would encompass Lebanon and Gaza, linking the Hormuz opening to broader regional stability. While the White House said constructive discussions are underway through mediators, Gulf Arab states have offered no immediate response, and Saudi Arabia remains skeptical. Analysts note that the proposal mirrors the June Memorandum of Understanding but compresses the previously 60‑day negotiation window into a week, raising doubts about feasibility before the U.S. mid‑term elections in November.

The Strait of Hormuz has become Tehran’s primary leverage, as the blockade has crippled Iran’s oil exports and pressured its economy. Conversely, the United States holds the leverage of naval control over the chokepoint. Both sides appear unwilling to be the first to relinquish this leverage, making the seven‑day timetable a high‑stakes diplomatic gamble. If successful, the deal could ease global oil market tensions, reduce regional military risks, and shift the political calculus for the upcoming U.S. elections.
