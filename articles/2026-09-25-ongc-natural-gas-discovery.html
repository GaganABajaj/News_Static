ONGC’s Deep‑water Well Off Odisha Begins Flowing Natural Gas

India’s state‑run Oil and Natural Gas Corporation (ONGC) hit a gas‑bearing reservoir while drilling the MN‑DW18‑1‑H‑D well in the Mahanadi Offshore Basin, about 43 km southeast of Konark, Odisha. The well has been flowing gas continuously for three days, showing encouraging pressure. While the discovery could fast‑track development because of its proximity to the coast, ONGC cautions that full commercial viability will be decided only after detailed appraisal and resource evaluation.

By GaganPublished September 25, 2026 · 11:22 PM
AI Image

What Happened?

  • ONGC reported that the deep‑water well MN‑DW18‑1‑H‑D struck natural gas on 18 September 2024.
  • The well is flowing gas continuously for three days, with “encouraging flow and reservoir pressure,” according to the company.
  • Drilled to a target depth of 1,623 m, the gas zone was encountered between 1,441 m and 1,452 m.

Where?

  • The discovery is located in the Mahanadi Offshore Basin, a deep‑water block off India’s eastern coast.
  • It lies roughly 43 km (≈ 27 miles) southeast of the historic town of Konark, Odisha.

When?

  • Drilling of the well began on 25 July 2024 using the drillship Platinum Explorer.
  • The gas hit was recorded on 18 September 2024, and the flow has continued for three days as of the report (early October 2024).

Why Is It Important?

  • Strategic proximity: The short distance to the Odisha shoreline could enable faster, lower‑cost development if the reservoir proves commercial.
  • Shared‑facility potential: ONGC highlighted that the find, together with nearby discoveries, could be developed using common infrastructure under the Petroleum and Natural Gas Rules, 2025, reducing capital expenditure and accelerating timelines.
  • Energy security: The discovery aligns with India’s broader push to increase domestic hydro‑carbon production and reduce reliance on imported gas.

What’s Next?

ONGC’s roadmap includes: 1. Further appraisal of the reservoir to delineate its size and quality. 2. Assessment of recoverable resources (estimating volumes of gas in place). 3. Full commercial evaluation to decide whether to move to development.

Only after these steps will a final decision on field development be announced.
